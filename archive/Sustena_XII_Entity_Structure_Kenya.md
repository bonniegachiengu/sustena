# SECTION 1: OVERVIEW OF KENYAN BUSINESS ENTITY TYPES

## 1.1 Entity Options at a Glance

Kenya's Companies Act 2015 (Cap 486) and related legislation provide several entity structures. The right choice depends on liability needs, investor readiness, regulatory requirements, and governance complexity.

| Entity Type | Liability | Min. Members | Share Issuance | Investor Ready | Recommended For |
|------------|-----------|-------------|----------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Sole Proprietorship** | Unlimited | 1 | No | No | Single-founder micro businesses |
| **Partnership** | Unlimited | 2-20 | No | No | Professional services |
| **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** | Limited | 2+ | No | Limited | Professional firms |
| **Private Limited Company (Ltd)** | Limited | 1-50 | Yes (private) | Yes | Startups, most businesses |
| **Public Limited Company (PLC)** | Limited | 7+ | Yes (public) | Yes (listed) | Post-Series B, listed companies |
| **Company Limited by Guarantee** | Limited | 1+ | No (guarantees) | No | NGOs, associations |

## 1.2 Why Private Limited Company (Ltd) is Right for Sustena

For Sustena Technologies, Colosso Finance, and Vyyb Operations, the Private Limited Company is the correct entity type because:

1. **Limited liability:** Founders' personal assets protected from company debts
2. **Share issuance:** Can issue shares to investors, ESOP holders, and strategic partners
3. **Compliance pathway:** Meets requirements for future CBK, CMA, and other sectoral licences
4. **DAO overlay:** Best compatible structure for layering DAO governance onto a legally recognized entity
5. **Professional credibility:** Banks, vendors, and enterprise clients prefer dealing with Ltd companies
6. **Tax efficiency:** Corporate tax (30%) + dividend distribution is often more efficient than personal income tax at scale

## 1.3 The 365+ Holdings Structure

The Sustena group uses a **holding company + subsidiaries** model:

```
365+ HOLDINGS LTD
(holding company -- owns shares in all subsidiaries)
     |
     +-- Sustena Technologies Ltd (the platform -- IP, runtime, Mycelium)
     |
     +-- Colosso Finance Ltd (financial sustain -- personal finance OS)
     |
     +-- Vyyb Operations Ltd (F&B sustain -- restaurant/business OS)
     |
     +-- Homestead Technologies Ltd (household sustain -- Phase 3+)
     |
     +-- [Future sustains as new Ltd companies]
```

**Why a holding company?**
- Clean separation: each sustain raises its own capital without diluting the others
- IP protection: Sustena Technologies owns all core IP; licenses to subsidiaries
- Tax efficiency: intra-group dividends from subsidiary to holding company are tax-exempt in Kenya
- Investor clarity: sector-specific investors back specific sustains, not the whole group
- Exit optionality: individual sustains can be sold or listed independently

- Cost: KES 20,000-50,000 from a commercial lawyer
- Timeline: 5-10 business days for drafting

**For Phase 1 (speed priority):** Use Table A Articles
**For Phase 2 (investor readiness):** Commission custom M&A

## 3.5 Shareholder Agreement (SHA)

The Shareholder Agreement is a private contract between all shareholders that supplements the Articles of Association. It is not filed publicly (unlike the M&A).

**Key SHA provisions for Sustena group:**

### Reserved Matters (require 75% shareholder approval)
- Issuing new shares or changing share capital
- Acquiring or disposing of assets worth >20% of company value
- Taking on debt exceeding KES 5,000,000
- Changing the company's principal business
- Entering related-party transactions
- Winding up or dissolving the company

### Founder Protections
- **Vesting schedule:** Founders' shares vest over 4 years (25% cliff at 12 months, then monthly)
- **Leaver provisions:** If a founder leaves before vesting, unvested shares return to the company
- **Non-compete:** 12 months post-departure within Sustena's market (AI-powered personal finance in Kenya)
- **Deadlock resolution:** If board is deadlocked (50/50), first mediation (14 days), then binding arbitration

### DAO Integration Provisions
- **Council Advisory Rights:** The Council (as defined in the Sustena XII Master Strategy) has the right to submit non-binding recommendations on Reserved Matters to the Board
- **Token Governance:** Holders of Sustena Tokens (STC) may submit proposals via the DAO interface, which the Board will consider but is not bound by (until Kenya's DAO law evolves)
- **Smart Contract Resolutions:** Board resolutions passed via the DAO smart contract voting mechanism shall be treated as equivalent to written resolutions under the Companies Act, provided they are signed by all directors within 7 days

### Information Rights
- Monthly management accounts to all shareholders
- Annual audited accounts within 120 days of financial year end
- Board observer rights for any shareholder holding >5% of shares

# SECTION 4: DAO GOVERNANCE OVERLAY

## 4.1 Legal Status of DAOs in Kenya (2026)

As of 2026, Kenya has not enacted specific DAO legislation. The Capital Markets Authority (CMA) has been developing a regulatory sandbox for digital assets but has not finalized DAO-specific rules. The Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) has issued guidance on virtual assets but not on DAO governance structures.

**Legal position:** DAOs are not recognized as legal persons in Kenya. Therefore, Sustena's DAO governance must operate within and alongside the existing company law framework.

**Strategic approach:** Build the DAO governance system as a **powerful internal tool** that informs and executes company decisions -- but always maintain the legal company structure as the binding layer.

## 4.2 The Hybrid Governance Architecture

```
LAYER 1: KENYAN COMPANY LAW (binding)
  - Companies Act 2015
  - Memorandum & Articles of Association
  - Shareholders' Agreement
  - Board of Directors (legal authority)

LAYER 2: DAO GOVERNANCE (advisory -> increasingly binding)
  - The Council (operative-level DAO)
  - Token-weighted voting on proposals
  - Smart contract execution of approved proposals
  - On-chain audit trail

LAYER 3: TRANSLATION MECHANISM
  - DAO votes -> Board resolutions (all directors sign)
  - Board resolutions -> Company statutory books
  - Smart contract escrow -> Company bank escrow (parallel)
```

## 4.3 The Council's Legal Standing

The Council (as defined in the Master Strategy) operates as a **Board Advisory Committee** under Section 207(2) of the Companies Act 2015, which allows the board to delegate specific functions to committees.

**Board resolution to establish The Council Committee:**
```
RESOLVED THAT:
The Council Advisory Committee is hereby established with the following terms of reference:
1. To review and score all strategic proposals submitted by the Orchestrator Operative
2. To recommend preferred strategies to the Board by weighted vote
3. To monitor implementation of approved strategies
4. To submit monthly governance reports to the Board
The Committee shall have no authority to bind the Company without Board approval.
```

This makes The Council's deliberations **legally recognized advisory activity** -- not just an AI experiment.

## 4.4 Token Governance Implementation

**Phase 1 (Months 1-12): Off-chain simulation**
- DAO votes tracked in database (not on blockchain)
- Results treated as advisory recommendations
- No real tokens issued
- Legal effect: zero (advisory only)

**Phase 2 (Months 13-24): Testnet governance**
- Governance tokens minted on Polygon testnet (no real value)
- Voting interface built and tested
- Results submitted as Board proposals
- Legal effect: zero (testnet; advisory only)

**Phase 3 (Months 25-36): Mainnet governance**
- STC tokens issued on mainnet with governance rights
- Token holders can submit and vote on proposals
- Results submitted as Board proposals for ratification
- Legal effect: Non-binding on the company; binding within the DAO's private agreement

**Phase 4 (Month 37+): Hybrid binding governance**
- Custom SHA amended to give certain token votes the same force as shareholder votes
- Specific decision classes (Mycelium listings, Operative approvals) delegated fully to DAO
- Legal effect: Binding for delegated matters; company law governs the rest

## 4.5 Smart Contract-to-Company Integration

For each DAO vote that affects company operations, a three-step translation process:

**Step 1: DAO vote completes**
- Smart contract records vote result on-chain
- Threshold met -> proposal marked "passed"

**Step 2: Board ratification (same-day target)**
- Orchie generates a draft Board resolution from the DAO vote
- All directors receive the resolution via email (signed electronically via DocuSign or similar)
- Signed resolution stored in company statutory books

**Step 3: Execution**
- Smart contract executes programmatic elements (e.g., release escrowed funds)
- Manual elements executed by designated director or officer
- Execution recorded in both the on-chain event log and the company's accounting records

# SECTION 5: REGULATORY COMPLIANCE IN DEPTH

## 5.1 Central Bank of Kenya (CBK)

**Relevant legislation:**
- Banking Act (Cap 488)
- National Payment System Act 2011
- Central Bank of Kenya Act (Cap 491)
- CBK Guidance Note on Virtual Asset Service Providers (2023)

**CBK triggers and actions:**

| Activity | CBK Trigger | Licence Required | Timeline |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| Displaying or storing user account balances | Not a trigger -- informational only | None | Immediately allowed |
| Receiving M-Pesa data via API (read-only) | Not a trigger -- Safaricom's licence covers this | None | Immediately allowed |
| Aggregating M-Pesa payments on behalf of users | YES -- payment aggregation | Payment Service Provider (PSP) | Phase 3 |
| Offering digital credit to users | YES -- credit provision | Digital Credit Provider (DCP) | Phase 3 |
| Holding user funds in a pool | YES -- deposit-taking | Deposit-Taking Microfinance Licence or Banking Licence | NOT in Sustena's plan |
| Operating a chama's shared account | YES -- if Sustena holds the account | As above | NOT in Sustena's plan |

**Phase 1-2 safe harbour:**
Sustena and Colosso operate purely as **software services and financial analytics tools**. Users' money stays in users' M-Pesa accounts and bank accounts. Sustena never holds, pools, or controls user funds. This keeps Sustena outside CBK's jurisdiction for Phases 1 and 2.

**Phase 3 regulatory strategy:**
- Obtain Digital Credit Provider (DCP) licence for any lending features
- Partner with a licensed PSP (e.g., Cellulant, DPO Group) for payment aggregation rather than self-licensing initially
- CBK DCP application: KES 50,000 application fee + minimum capital of KES 3,000,000

## 5.2 Capital Markets Authority (CMA)

**Relevant legislation:**
- Capital Markets Act (Cap 485A)
- Capital Markets (Collective Investment Schemes) Regulations 2001
- CMA Digital Assets Policy (ongoing)

**CMA triggers and actions:**

| Activity | CMA Trigger | Licence Required | Timeline |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| Providing investment information/education | NO -- informational only | None | Immediately allowed |
| Recommending specific securities to buy | YES -- investment advice | Investment Adviser licence | Phase 3 |
| Managing a pool of investments for users | YES -- fund management | Fund Manager licence | NOT in Phase 1-2 |
| Issuing tokens that represent equity or profit sharing | YES -- securities offering | Approval from CMA | Phase 4 (with careful structuring) |
| Providing access to licensed fund products (e.g., CIC MMF) | NO -- distribution only if partnered | None (partner handles) | Phase 2 |

**Safe harbour for Sustena tokens (STC):**
STC must be structured as **utility tokens** (access to services) NOT as investment/equity tokens:
- STC purchases compute credits (access to Sustena's AI compute)
- STC pays for Mycelium operator usage
- STC is NOT marketed as an investment
- STC holders have NO claim on Sustena's profits or assets
- This keeps STC outside CMA's securities regulation framework

**If STC is deemed a security despite utility structuring:**
- CMA has a regulatory sandbox for digital assets
- Apply for sandbox inclusion -- get 2-year regulatory accommodation
- Use sandbox period to negotiate permanent regulatory treatment

|--------------|---------|-------------|----------|

### 5.4.2 ODPC Registration

**Application process:**
1. Go to odpc.go.ke -> Registration -> Data Controller Registration
2. Fill in: Organization details, categories of personal data processed, purposes of processing, security measures, cross-border transfer details
3. Upload: Certificate of Incorporation, Privacy Policy (must be published on website/app)
4. Pay KES 5,000 via M-Pesa or bank transfer
5. Receive registration certificate (valid 2 years; renewable)

**Annual renewal:** KES 2,500

### 5.4.3 Privacy Policy Requirements

The Privacy Policy must cover:
1. Identity and contact details of the data controller (Sustena Technologies Ltd)
2. Identity and contact details of the DPO (Data Protection Officer)
3. Categories of personal data collected
4. Purpose and legal basis for each processing activity
5. Recipients of personal data (third parties, e.g., Claude API, M-Pesa)
6. International transfers (e.g., data sent to Anthropic in the US -- requires adequacy decision or SCCs)
7. Retention periods
8. Data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection, automated decision-making
9. Right to lodge a complaint with ODPC
10. Whether providing data is a contractual/statutory requirement

### 5.4.4 Data Subject Rights (Operational Implementation)

| Right | User's Entitlement | Sustena's Obligation | Implementation |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Access | Copy of all personal data held | Provide within 21 days of request | Orchie command: "Export my data" |
| Rectification | Correct inaccurate data | Update within 14 days | Orchie command: "Update my details" |
| Erasure | Delete personal data | Delete within 30 days (subject to legal retention obligations) | Orchie command: "Delete my account" |
| Portability | Receive data in machine-readable format | Provide JSON/CSV export | Orchie command: "Download my data" |
| Objection | Object to processing for direct marketing | Honor immediately; must stop | Orchie: "Stop marketing messages" |

### 5.4.5 Cross-Border Data Transfer (Claude API)

Every time Sustena sends user data to Claude's API, it constitutes a cross-border data transfer (Anthropic is US-based).

**Compliance approach:**
- Include in Privacy Policy: "We use Claude (Anthropic, USA) to process your queries. Data is transmitted to the United States under standard contractual clauses."
- Enter into a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with Anthropic (Anthropic's standard DPA available for API customers)
- Ensure user consent covers this transfer at onboarding
- Minimize data sent to Claude: never send National ID numbers, bank account numbers, or unnecessary personal identifiers -- send only what's needed to answer the query

### 5.4.6 Security Requirements

DPA 2019 requires "appropriate technical and organizational measures":

**Technical measures (Phase 1):**
- Encryption at rest: SQLite database with SQLCipher encryption
- Encryption in transit: TLS 1.3 for all API connections
- Access controls: Role-based access; founders only in Phase 1
- Audit logging: All data access logged with timestamp and user
- Pseudonymization: User IDs in analytics (never raw names or phone numbers)

**Organizational measures:**
- Privacy policy published before first user onboarded
- Staff (Phase 2+) trained on DPA obligations
- Breach response plan documented: who to notify (ODPC within 72 hours), how to assess impact, how to notify affected users

### 5.4.7 Breach Notification Protocol

If a data breach occurs:

1. **Hour 0-2:** Identify and contain the breach (isolate affected system)
2. **Hour 2-4:** Assess scope (what data, how many users, what risk)
3. **Within 72 hours:** Notify ODPC (mandatory if breach poses risk to data subjects)
   - Notification must include: nature of breach, categories and number of data subjects, likely consequences, measures taken/proposed
4. **Within 7 days:** Notify affected users (if breach is likely to result in high risk to them)
5. **Within 30 days:** Submit full incident report to ODPC

# SECTION 6: CHAMA LEGAL MODEL

## 6.1 Legal Status of Chamas in Kenya

Kenyan chamas (self-help groups and investment clubs) can exist in several legal forms:

| Form | Governing Law | Registration Body | Suitable For |
|------|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Unregistered informal group | None (contract law only) | N/A | Small merry-go-round groups |
| Registered Society | Societies Act (Cap 108) | Registrar of Societies | General chamas; welfare groups |
| Company Limited by Guarantee | Companies Act 2015 | Business Registration Service | Formal investment chamas |
| SACCO | Co-operative Societies Act | Commissioner for Co-operative Development | Savings and credit co-operatives |
| Investment Club (under CMA) | Capital Markets Act | CMA (informal guidance) | Investment-focused chamas |

## 6.2 Sustena's Position as Chama Secretary

**Critical legal distinction:** Sustena's Chama Secretary Operative is a **software service that tracks and coordinates** -- NOT a financial institution or fund manager.

What Chama Secretary DOES (legally safe):
- Record contributions (informational -- not holding funds)
- Track who has paid and who owes (accounting function)
- Calculate rotation schedules (computational function)
- Generate audit trail and reports (secretarial function)
- Facilitate member communication (communication function)
- Record loan book entries (accounting function -- tracking amounts)
- Calculate interest owed (mathematical function)

What Chama Secretary does NOT do (legally required):
- Hold chama funds in any Sustena-controlled account
- Process actual M-Pesa payments (users initiate their own payments)
- Act as signatory on any chama bank account
- Provide legal advice to chama members
- Guarantee any contribution or loan

**The Chama Secretary's M-Pesa matching:** When the system detects an M-Pesa message matching an expected chama contribution, it updates the tracking record. The money moves between individual M-Pesa accounts -- Sustena merely observes and records the confirmation message.

## 6.3 Chama Registration Advisory Service

Sustena's Chama Secretary Operative will include an advisory module on chama registration:

**For chamas wanting to register under the Societies Act:**
1. Draft constitution (Sustena provides template)
2. Obtain KES 1,000 application fee
3. Submit Form HS-1 to Registrar of Societies
4. Processing: 14-30 days
5. Receive Certificate of Registration
6. Annual return: KES 500

**Benefits of registration:**
- Legal recognition: can open bank accounts, enter contracts, own property as a group
- Limited liability for members (if structured correctly)
- Access to government programs (Women Enterprise Fund, Youth Enterprise Fund)
- Easier to access formal credit

## 8.1 Company Bank Accounts

**For each entity, open:**

2. **Fixed Deposit or MMF** -- emergency fund / excess cash management
3. **Salary Account** -- staff payroll (when hiring begins)

**Recommended banks for tech startups in Kenya:**

- **KCB:** Solid corporate banking; KCB API integration with M-Pesa
- **Co-operative Bank:** Good for SMEs; lower fees
- **NCBA:** Strong digital capabilities; preferred by some fintech startups
- **Absa:** Good for international transactions (remittances, USD accounts)

**Required for account opening (company account):**
- Certificate of Incorporation
- Company KRA PIN
- M&A (or Table A confirmation)
- Board resolution authorizing account opening and naming signatories
- Directors' KRA PINs and IDs
- Business permit

**Processing time:** 3-10 business days
**Minimum balance:** KES 5,000-10,000 (varies by bank)

## 8.2 M-Pesa Paybill / Till Setup

For Vyyb and Colosso (Phase 2+):

**Paybill Number:**
- Apply via Safaricom Business: Safaricom Business -> M-Pesa -> Apply for Paybill
- Required: Business registration docs + authorized signatory details
- Fees: KES 500 setup + KES 1.5-3% transaction fee
- Processing: 5-10 business days
- Use: Customer payments, subscription billing (Colosso)

**Till Number (for Vyyb POS):**
- Apply for Lipa na M-Pesa Till
- Required: Business registration docs
- Fees: No setup fee; KES 0 for transactions below KES 100; standard charges above
- Use: POS payments at Vyyb

**Pochi la Biashara (already used in Vyyb):**
- Personal M-Pesa linked to business -- no formal registration
- Suitable for Phase 1 small-scale transactions
- Limitation: No API access; manual reconciliation only

## 8.3 USD Account for International Transactions

For API costs (Claude, cloud services priced in USD), Anthropic billing:
- Open a USD current account at Equity Bank or Absa
- Link to a Visa/Mastercard business debit card
- For small purchases (<$100/month): use founders' personal cards and reimburse

**Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI):** kipi.go.ke

**Process:**
1. Trademark search (verify no existing similar marks): KES 1,000
2. File trademark application (Form TM-1): KES 4,000 per class per mark
3. Examination: 3-6 months
4. Publication in Kenya Gazette: 3 months opposition period
5. Registration certificate: KES 4,000
6. Total cost per mark: ~KES 9,000 + agent fees if using a lawyer
