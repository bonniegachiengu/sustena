**In short**

- A household, a savings pocket, a kitchen, a business — each has to hold itself inside a safe range, and none of them stays there on its own.
- Sustena treats all of them as one object: a **Sustain** — a system that declares the range it must stay in and allows only the moves that keep it there.
- Everything is built from that one object, nested: a pocket inside a household inside a business, and the whole never overwrites its parts.
- The oldest version of it is a living cell, which has run on the same five parts for four billion years.

---

A household has to hold itself together: rent covered, food in, no account underwater. Keeping it that way is daily work — money comes and goes, and a payment can always land late — and if the work stops, the whole thing comes apart.

Sustena is built to do that work, for a household and for anything shaped the same way. This article is about the object it uses, because it is the only object Sustena has.

---

#### **One system, one safe range**

Start with the smallest example: a savings pocket. It holds a balance, and it has one rule: the balance never goes below zero. Ask it to release more than it holds and the move doesn't happen. The rule is built into what the pocket is.

The same shape is everywhere. A kitchen holds stock, and none of it can go negative. A household holds a month — rent, food, a few accounts — and "fine" means every one of those numbers is sitting where it should. A business holds a season.

Each is the same shape: some numbers, a declared range they must stay inside, and a hard edge that won't let them leave. Sustena calls that shape a **Sustain**, and builds everything from it. A Sustain has five parts, and you already met all five in the pocket.

#### **The boundary — what it owns**

The boundary says what belongs to the Sustain: which numbers and which members it is responsible for holding. The pocket owns one balance. A household owns its members' pockets, its stock, its calendar. Drawing the boundary is how a system says what it has to keep in range, and what falls outside its care.

#### **The state — what's true now**

The state is everything true inside the boundary at this moment: the balances, the stock, the calendar, the readings. It is the one source of what's real for this Sustain, and it is built from the record — the balance is what you get by adding up every deposit and spend, in order. Because it is the sum of its whole history, the state can always be rebuilt and checked. A number that can't be traced back to something that happened is a number to distrust.

#### **The viable region — the safe range**

The viable region is where the state is allowed to be: balance ≥ 0, no pocket below zero, no stock negative, plus the rules that tie those together. It marks a whole region to live inside — a band each value must stay within, and the ties between them. While the state sits inside the region, the Sustain is healthy; step outside it and something is wrong by definition. The point is that Sustena makes you *declare* this range — write it down as the system's law — so "is this household okay?" becomes a question a machine can answer exactly.

#### **The transitions — the only moves, each with a price**

Nothing changes the state except a defined move: a transfer, a spend, a shopping trip. Every move passes one gate, and the gate allows it only if the move is permitted and the result still lands inside the safe range. Fail either test and the move doesn't happen. Sustena calls these guarded moves **Enzymes** (they get their own article), and each one costs something — money, time, compute — because changing a system's state is never free.

That single gate is the whole payoff. Declare the range and the moves once, and the wrong state never happens, because no move that reaches it is ever allowed through — whether by accident or on purpose at 2 a.m. when no one is watching. The gate stops the wrong state from arriving in the first place.

#### **Composition — they nest, and the whole keeps its parts**

Sustains stack. A pocket sits inside a household; a household sits inside a business. A parent's state is its children's states rolled up together, and a child's safe range has to fit inside the parent's. One rule holds this together and can never break: the whole is real, and it never overwrites the parts inside it. A household's total is the sum of its members' pockets — it never reaches in and rewrites a pocket to balance its own books.

#### **The oldest one**

None of this is new. The oldest Sustain there is runs in every cell of your body. A living cell holds a difference against the world — sodium out, potassium in — and spends energy every second to keep it; stop paying, and the difference dissolves, the cell swells, and it dies. Its boundary is the membrane, its safe range is the chemistry it keeps within, its moves are its pumps, and its cost is real fuel. Four billion years, the same five parts. Sustena took that ancient object and wrote it down, so a household could use it.

#### **The harder target**

One part matters more than the rest. Being inside the safe range right now is weaker than it looks; what counts is whether you can *stay* inside it. A balance that's fine today with rent due Thursday is already in trouble — no move is left that keeps it in range. So Sustena aims past "in the range now" for the smaller region you can actually hold from: the states that still have a way forward. A household watching a thin week is asking one thing — is there still a way through to the end of the month?

---

#### **What you have is not what is in the account**

A cell is not the sum of its free-floating molecules. Most of what it is made of is *committed* — built into membrane, bound up in protein, held as a store it will draw on later. Count only what is dissolved and floating and you would conclude a healthy cell was starving.

Households are the same and it catches people out constantly. Money leaves the account on Saturday and comes back as a full cupboard. Money leaves on Monday and comes back as nothing, because it paid the electricity. Those are not the same event, and an app that shows you one falling number for both is telling you they are.

So the state of a household has to distinguish kinds of thing. What money **is** — sitting in M-Pesa, sitting in a bank. What it has **become** — food, gas, a claim on somebody who will pay you back. What is **owed** to somebody else. And, separately, what money is *for*, which is a plan and not a possession.

That last distinction is the one that quietly ruins arithmetic. The thousand shillings you have set aside for food are the same thousand shillings your M-Pesa balance already holds. Count the plan and the money and you have counted your shillings twice — and a household that budgets carefully would appear, on paper, to be twice as rich as one that does not.

---

A savings pocket, a kitchen, a household, a business, a living cell — one object at every scale. Each owns a boundary, holds a state, declares a safe range, changes only through gated moves, and nests inside something larger without being erased by it. Learn it once and you can read all of them. That is the whole of Sustena: the constant work of holding yourself together, done out loud where you can see it, in a system that won't let you quietly fall out of range.
