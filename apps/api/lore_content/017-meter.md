#### **A machine that charges itself for thinking**

In 2015 a strange new kind of computer switched on. It had no single owner, sat in no single building, and ran on thousands of machines at once that had never met. Ethereum could run any program a person cared to write — but it added one rule nobody's laptop had ever enforced: **every single step of computation had a price, paid before the step was allowed to run.**

They called the price *gas*. Add two numbers, pay a little gas. Store a value forever, pay much more. Run a wasteful program, watch the meter drain until you either finished or ran dry. And the coin you paid with — *ether* — was not a share of a company or a bet on a founder. It was fuel. You held it to *use* the machine, the way you hold a full tank to drive.

That one rule — *put a price on each thought, and make the user carry the fuel* — quietly solved a problem every shared computer eventually hits. Who gets to run what, for how long, without one greedy program starving everyone else? You don't need a committee. You need a meter and a fuel gauge.

Sustena needs the same rule, for the same reason — and it turns out the meter is useful long before there's ever a coin.

---

#### **Two words, borrowed honestly**

Sustena's version has two names, and keeping them separate keeps the whole idea clear.

- **Juul is the coin.** It is Sustena's *ether* — the utility token you hold to use the network. Not a share. Not an investment vehicle. Fuel.
- **Pawa is the gas.** It is the *cost of doing* — the compute and storage an action actually consumes, measured, and paid in Juul.

The Swahili word *pawa* is a loan from *power*, and that is exactly right: pawa is the power a system spends to change its own state. An Enzyme fires; it burns some pawa. A sustain stores a year of events; it holds some pawa in reserve. Nothing moves in a Sustena network for free, because nothing moves anywhere for free — a cell spends ATP to stay alive, a server spends watts to answer, and pretending otherwise is how shared systems quietly rot.

---

#### **The meter comes first**

Here is the part that matters today, before any token exists.

The moment you *measure* pawa — the moment every Enzyme run records the compute and storage it actually used — you have something valuable in your hands whether or not there is ever a coin:

- You can finally answer *"how much computing and storage do I need?"* — not by guessing, but by reading the meter.
- You can see which Enzymes are cheap and which are expensive, and design accordingly.
- And you can compare two ways of doing the same thing, and know which one wastes less.

That last one is the quiet superpower. When Sustena's advisors sit down to plan — when several **competing strategies** are laid on the table for the same goal — pawa becomes the tie-breaker with teeth. Two plans reach the same future; one burns half the pawa. The efficient one wins, and now "efficient" isn't a feeling, it's a number the simulator hands you before you commit a single real action.

Gas, it turns out, is not just a toll. It is a *measure of grace* — of how lightly a system can achieve what it set out to do.

---

#### **The economy that grows on top**

Once there is a meter and a coin, an economy becomes possible — and it is the kind Sustena was always reaching for.

Sustena is meant to be built *together*. People will contribute pieces to a shared library: a sustain someone designed, an Enzyme someone wrote, a Symbiont someone trained, a widget someone shaped. Some of those pieces a person will give away — a **free licence**, the gift that grows the commons. Others they will offer under a **royalty licence** — *use my piece, and a little Juul flows back to me each time it runs.*

That is the whole trick of a healthy open ecosystem, and Ethereum's own foundation is the proof of shape: open primitives, a token that funds the people who build them, and an economy that grows around the work rather than around a single company. A contributor in Nairobi writes a brilliant Enzyme; a household in Kisumu uses it; Juul moves the short distance between them; and the meter — pawa — is what makes that fair, because it knows exactly what was used.

---

#### **A shared purse for the commons**

Some of the best work in any commons pays off slowly, or belongs to no one in particular: the research, the shared infrastructure, the free pieces everyone builds on, the grant that lets a contributor keep going before their work earns its keep. So Sustena keeps a **treasury** — a shared purse held by the network itself, funded in Juul, spent on the things the whole community needs.

The elegant part is that the purse plays by the same rules as everything else. A treasury *is* a Sustain: it holds balances, it can allocate and grant and disburse, and it has a viable region it must stay inside — keep a reserve floor, don't let any one line run away, never spend past what's in hand. Its budget is set in the open, category by category. And every payment out passes the same gate every action passes, which means a person signs off on it. Sustena funds its own commons with the very machine it hands everyone else — the network keeping its own books as a Sustain. Where does the purse fill from? A slice of the fees, a slice of the royalties, a share set aside at the start — each one declared out loud, and each one adjustable later.

---

#### **How a thing like this switches on**

A network with no owner still has to *begin*. Ethereum's whitepaper sketches how, and Sustena borrows the shape in its own words.

It starts with a **first, stated moment** — who holds how much Juul on day one, written down in the open for anyone to check. From there, new Juul enters in one honest way: it is **earned by the machines that do the work.** A host that lends its compute and storage to the network is serving real pawa, and it is paid in Juul for exactly the service it rendered — the same idea as mining or staking, priced by the meter the system already trusts. Users spend Juul to run their thoughts; hosts earn Juul to run them; and because the meter sits on both sides, the incentives simply line up. The same out-of-fuel rule that stops a runaway program is also what keeps a flood of junk work expensive for whoever tries it.

And it grows outward without ever changing its mind. The meter, the coin, the efficiency tie-breaker, and the royalty flow are all defined on **one machine first**, where they earn their keep immediately. Then a few trusted machines share the load. Then the doors open, and value settles across hosts that never met. What was true of one host's little economy stays true of the whole network's — nothing gets redefined on the way out.

---

#### **What comes next**

The formal companion makes it precise: pawa as metered resource cost, Juul as the settlement unit, the efficiency ranking of competing strategies as a fitness term, contribution licences — free and royalty — as programmable flows of the token, the treasury as a Sustain that governs the commons, and a decentralized launch — genesis, issuance, hosts earning the coin — drawn in Sustena's own terms. It also draws the honest line: the *system* — the meter, the ledger, the royalty and treasury accounting — is software we can build now; issuing a real, public token and settling real value is a later, deliberate, human-authorized step.

But the first brick is small and buildable today, and it earns its keep immediately: **a meter that watches every Enzyme and tells you what it cost.**

Put a price on a thought, and the whole system learns to think well.

That is a Sustain. That is Sustena.
