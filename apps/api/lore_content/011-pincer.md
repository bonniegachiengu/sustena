- Planning forward is contaminated by present bias, present constraints, present fear.
- So invert it. Stand in a chosen future and look back at today.

- Decide the hard calls now, while you're calm. Leave them for your future self.

---

You've been briefed already. You just don't know it yet.

That's not mysticism. That's the core insight behind Tenet's central mechanism — and behind every serious strategic decision-making system ever built. The future has already happened in simulation. Your job is to read it backwards, extract the signal, and let it rewrite what you do today.

This is the algorithm.

*A note on the name.* Tenet is named for Christopher Nolan's *Tenet*, where a mission runs as a "temporal pincer" — one team moving forward through it, another backward from its end, meeting in the middle (Step 8, here). The film supplies the imagery; the engine underneath is [backward induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_induction) — reasoning from a fixed future back to your next move, the logic chess engines and [backcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backcasting) planners already use.

---

#### **The premise**

Most people plan forward. They stand in the present, look ahead, and try to predict. The problem: prediction from the present is contaminated by present bias, present constraints, present fear. You plan around what you have, not around what you need.

Temporal inversion flips this. You stand in a simulated future and look backward at the present. From there, the path is visible in a way it never is from the starting point. You're not predicting anymore. You're reading a map that already exists.

A **Symbiont** — a goal-driven agent that lives inside a Sustena system and works one corner of it, covered in its own article — doesn't ask *what will happen.* The Symbiont asks *what had to be true for this outcome to occur* — and then goes and makes those things true.

---

#### **The algorithm**

#### **Step 1 — Define the Inversion Point**

Pick a future moment with enough specificity to be real. Not "success" — that's not a location. A specific state. Named. Dated. Measurable.

*Example: "It is eighteen months from now. My café is open, covers its costs, and pays me a wage. I run it from one leased space and keep three months of expenses in reserve."*

That's an inversion point. You can stand there and look back.

---

#### **Step 2 — Run Three Futures**

Never simulate one future. One future is a plan. Three futures is intelligence.

Simulate:
- **Future A — Best case.** The café finds its regulars within weeks. Revenue climbs steadily. No major shocks.
- **Future B — Base case.** A slow first season. One significant unexpected cost. Revenue builds late.
- **Future C — Worst case.** Footfall stays thin. The reserve runs down before the café breaks even.

Each future is valid. Each future is happening somewhere in probability space right now. Your decisions determine which one you're moving toward.

---

#### **Step 3 — Map the Decision Nodes**

In each future, identify the exact moments where the path diverged. These are your decision nodes — the points in time where one action led to Future A and a different action led to Future C.

*Example decision nodes for the café:*
- The lease: a busy high-street unit vs. a cheaper side street → determines fixed costs for two years
- Launch timing: open lean this month vs. waiting to raise more → determines customer base and learning at month 3
- Staffing: hire early vs. run it solo at first → determines how fast cash burns before revenue lands

Decision nodes are where the algorithm does its most important work. Most people discover them after they've passed. The algorithm finds them in advance.

---

#### **Step 4 — Backward Induction**

Take Future A — the best case. Ask: *what had to be true at month 6 for this to be possible?* Write it down. Then: *what had to be true at month 3 for month 6 to be possible?* Then month 1. Then this week.

You are now looking at the present from the future. The actions required today are no longer guesses. They are conditions. Things that **must be true** for the future you chose to exist.

Do the same for Future C — the worst case. Map it backwards. Now you have a list of conditions that, if they become true, are signals that you're in the wrong future.

---

#### **Step 5 — Find the Overlapping Actions**

Across all three futures, some actions appear in all backward maps. These are **temporally invariant decisions** — things that move you forward regardless of which future manifests.

*For the café, the invariant actions are:*
- Open now, at small scale → builds regulars in all futures
- Keep fixed costs low → widens the runway in all futures
- Write down how everything is done from day one → makes hiring painless later, in all futures

These are your unconditional priorities. Execute them regardless of external conditions.

---

#### **Step 6 — Plant Dead Drops**

A dead drop in Tenet is a pre-positioned resource — placed by your future self for your present self to find. In the framework, dead drops are **pre-made decisions.**

When you reach a known decision node, you don't want to be deciding under pressure. You decide now, from a position of clarity, and leave the instruction for your future self.

*Example dead drops for the café:*
- *"If daily footfall is still under forty by month 4, I move to the cheaper unit. Decision already made."*
- *"If revenue covers rent and my wage two months running, I hire the first barista. No debate."*
- *"If a supplier locks me into a minimum I can't clear in a slow month, I decline regardless of pressure."*

Pre-made decisions eliminate negotiation with yourself under stress. Stress is a terrible advisor.

---

#### **Step 7 — Read the Future Signals**

Every future broadcasts early signals before it arrives. These are leading indicators — small present-tense facts that tell you which future you're moving toward.

Identify them now, while you're calm.

*Signals you're in Future A:*
- First paying customers in the opening week
- A repeat face within month 1
- A steady lunch crowd by month 2

*Signals you're drifting toward Future C:*
- Fewer than twenty customers a day by week 3
- Monthly costs outrunning revenue

When a signal fires, you don't panic. You were already briefed. You invert — change the present action that the signal is pointing at.

---

#### **Step 8 — The Temporal Pincer**

The most powerful move in the framework. You run two operations simultaneously: one moving forward from today, one moving backward from the target future. They meet in the middle.

Forward operation: execute daily actions — open up, serve, market, restock, watch the numbers.

Backward operation: every two weeks, re-enter the future simulation. Reassess which future you're in. Update the dead drops. Recalibrate the decision nodes based on new information.

Where the two operations meet is your real position — not where you hoped to be, not where you feared to be. Where you actually are. That's where the next action comes from.

---

#### **The rule**

The algorithm doesn't tell you what will happen.

It tells you what to do today so that when you arrive at the future, you recognize it — because you built it backwards, piece by piece, from a place that didn't exist yet.

*Don't try to understand it.*

*Use it.*

---

#### **In sum**

So the whole thing is one motion. You name a future exact enough to stand in, then run it three ways instead of one. You find the moments where the paths split, trace each one backward until it turns into something you can do this week, and start with the moves every future needs. You pre-decide the hard calls while you're calm, name the signals that tell you which future you're in, and keep running forward and backward at once, correcting as the real data comes in. Do that, and the future stops being something you brace for. It becomes something you're already walking into, on purpose.
