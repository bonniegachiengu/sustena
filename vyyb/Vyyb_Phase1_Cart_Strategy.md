## 0. THE PHASE 1 THESIS

**One Hive. One Outlet. Vyyb-Owned. Sustena-Tracked.**

The goal of Phase 1 is not to build a restaurant. It is to prove the modular franchise model — with Vyyb owning and operating the first outlet itself to validate the system before handing it to vendor partners.

- The Hive (commissary) produces everything. The outlet just finishes and sells.
- The first outlet is Vyyb-owned. This proves the model, generates real data, and trains the Vyyb.sustain system.
- Sustena tracks every dispatch, receipt, sale, and leftover from Day 1 — this data drives future logistics, pricing, and vendor partner matching.
- Once the model is proven, we replicate the outlet with vendor partners — universities, business parks, events, estates.

**Budget constraint: 500,000 – 600,000 KES total** (Hive setup + Cart + Uniforms + First stock)

**Design principles:**
- Jua Kali fabrication where safe and durable
- No walk-in freezer — rented space, everything removable
- No induction cooktops — gas burners only (lower cost, familiar to staff, no power dependency)
- Multipurpose equipment; no single-use items
- Eliminate plastic where possible — wood, SS, bamboo instead
- Scale what works; replace nothing until necessary
- Data at every step — every container labelled, every scan logged

## 3. THE MODULAR OUTLET STRUCTURE

### Philosophy

One armored modular kitchen. 3×2m footprint when assembled. Six interlocking modules roll into position, lock from the inside, and open into a fully functional finishing kitchen. Deploys in 30–45 minutes from module storage to service-ready. Closes into a sealed steel structure — weatherproof, secure, zero exterior fasteners — every night.

**Outlet aesthetic:** Fully branded across all exterior Alucobond cladding faces — full vinyl wrap in Vyyb safety orange. The four ground-level modules lock together into a sealed kibanda with smooth panel surfaces on all exterior faces. Not bare stainless steel — this is a brand asset on the street.

This is the franchise template. Every subsequent outlet is the same Jua Kali spec, same brand, same structure. Phase 1 proves it; Phase 2 replicates it.

For the full fabrication specification (frame gauges, hardware spec, module dimensions, external cage construction), see: `Technical Brief & Project Summary.md`

---

### Assembly Geometry

The four ground-level modules form a closed rectangle. Modules 1 and 3 are the two long faces (3m each, staff and customer). Modules 2 and 4 are the short sides that close both ends. The result is an enclosed interior workspace approximately 3m × 1m. Module 5 (solar roof + customer awning) mounts on telescopic legs above. Module 6 (floor panels) bridges the interior workspace floor.

All interlocking latches, toggle clamps, and connecting hardware are on the **interior face** of each module — no accessible fasteners on the exterior when closed. Module 4 (the door) is the only exterior access point, locked from outside at shutdown.

---

**Morning Mode (06:00 – 11:00) — Commuter Breakfast**

Both fryer baskets active. Full display running. Smoothie blending. Full menu.

**Event/Roaming Mode**
Modules disassemble and load onto a 1-ton pickup or multiple transport. Reassemble on-site — 30–45 minutes to service-ready.

## 5. BATCH CODE + QR SYSTEM — DATA FROM DAY 1

Every container dispatched from the Hive carries a QR-encoded batch label. This is Vyyb's first data layer — every scan is a timestamped event in Vyyb OS (and eventually Vyyb.sustain in Sustena).

### Label Format

**Human-readable:** `V-[ITEM CODE]-[DATE]-[BATCH]-[PREP TIME]`
Example: `V-CHK-260526-B02-0430`

**QR encodes:**
1. Same batch ID (for Sustena/Vyyb OS lookup)
2. Shelf life and storage instructions (text or link)
3. Prep/handling SOP link (internal, staff-facing)
4. Customer-facing page (if scanned publicly): app download + loyalty program + menu + payment

### Scan Events (Sustena Logs)

| Trigger | Who Scans | What Gets Logged |
|---------|----------|-----------------|
| Packing at Hive | Hub staff | Item, batch ID, container count, pack time, prep time |
| Dispatch | Hub staff | Dispatch time, transport method, recipient cart ID |
| Receipt at cart | Cart staff / vendor | Receipt time, temperature check (hot/cold), batch ID confirm |
| End-of-day | Cart staff | Items remaining by batch, returned containers, leftover food log |

All scan events feed Vyyb OS → Vyyb.sustain → analytics on: dispatch accuracy, cold chain integrity, food waste per item, demand forecasting.

### Cold Chain Rules

- **Cold containers (insulated cooler):** Must arrive ≤5°C. Staff checks temp on receipt, logs against batch.
- **Hot containers (insulated flask/SS):** Must arrive ≥60°C. Same log.
- Any container arriving without a valid QR batch label is **rejected** — logged as a cold chain breach incident.
- Empty containers + original QR labels return to Hive at end of day — Hub audits to confirm no outside sourcing.

### Connectivity at Cart

- 4G LTE Wi-Fi router (Safaricom/Airtel SIM, battery-backed) powers all cart devices.

## 7. VENDOR PARTNER MODEL

> *Phase 1: Vyyb operates the first cart directly to validate the model. Once proven, vendor partners run subsequent carts under this model.*
> *Revenue model confirmed: **Option A — Food Margin + Monthly Flat Fee.** Activates from Vendor #2 onward.*

### How it Works

1. Vyyb identifies a vendor partner — someone with a strategic pitch location (estate entrance, matatu stage, university gate, office park)
2. Vyyb provides: cart (on lease/facilitation), daily Hive supply, training, brand, Vyyb OS access
3. Vendor provides: location, labour, gas cylinders, local permits
4. **Revenue model: Option A — Food Margin + Monthly Flat Fee** *(confirmed)*

---

### Option A: Food Margin + Monthly Flat Fee

**The model has two revenue streams for Vyyb/Hive:**

#### Stream 1 — Food Margin (built into supply price)

Vyyb sells food to the vendor at a price above the Hive's cost of production. This markup is not arbitrary — it covers a defined stack of real costs:

| What the food markup covers | Rationale |
|-----------------------------|-----------|
| Ingredients + raw material cost | Direct input cost |
| Prep labour (Hub kitchen staff) | Time-value of Hub production |
| Packaging (containers, QR labels, wraps) | Per-unit consumable |
| QR/Sustena tech fee contribution | System infrastructure per batch |
| Hive overhead contribution | Shared space, utilities, equipment depreciation |
| Vyyb profit margin | The business model's core return |

The vendor buys from Vyyb at the supply price. They set their own retail selling price. The difference between what they sell for and what they pay Vyyb is their gross margin — they keep 100% of it.

This means Vyyb's food revenue is clean, predictable, and directly proportional to vendor volume. The more a vendor sells, the more food they buy from the Hive, and the more Vyyb earns on the food side.

#### Stream 2 — Monthly Flat Fee

The vendor pays a fixed monthly fee to Vyyb. This is not a royalty on sales — it is a flat access fee that covers:

| What the flat fee covers | Rationale |
|--------------------------|-----------|
| Cart facilitation and maintenance contribution | Cart is a Vyyb asset, maintained by Vyyb |
| Training amortization | Onboarding, SOP transfers, ramp-up support |
| Brand licensing | Vyyb name, identity, design system, uniform spec |
| Ongoing Sustena system access | Vyyb OS, QR batch system, demand prediction, analytics |

**Monthly flat fee: TBD after Phase 1 food cost audit.**

The fee is set once Phase 1 data reveals actual Hive unit economics — it will be calibrated to be affordable at low vendor volume and remain unchanged as the vendor scales.

---

### Why This Model is Symbiotic

**For the vendor:**
- They keep 100% of their sales revenue. There is no revenue-share or commission cut from their takings.
- The flat fee is fixed regardless of volume — so as the vendor sells more, their unit economics improve. Higher sales = same fixed fee = better margin per unit.
- The Sustena demand prediction system actively helps them order the right amount of food, reducing waste and improving their profitability.
- Incentive to grow is aligned: every additional unit they sell is pure upside for them.

**For Vyyb/Hive:**
- Food margin grows linearly with vendor sales volume — no cap.
- The flat fee provides predictable monthly revenue regardless of a vendor's daily fluctuations.
- As more vendor carts join, the Hive's fixed costs (equipment, lease, overhead) are spread across more total units → Hive margin improves with scale without any additional capital outlay.
- Incentive alignment: Vyyb benefits when vendors sell more, so Vyyb is motivated to give vendors the best possible supply quality, system support, and demand intelligence.

**The flywheel:**
More vendors → more Hive volume → lower Hive unit cost → better food supply economics → vendor unit economics improve → more vendors want to join.

### Phase 1 Exception — Vyyb-Owned First Outlet

The first cart is Vyyb-owned and operated directly. **No vendor fee applies to this outlet.** It is an internal test, not a vendor relationship. The food margin model is still applied internally for cost-tracking purposes (so Phase 1 data is comparable to future vendor economics), but there is no flat fee charged to Vyyb by itself.

**This model activates from Vendor #2 onward.**

### How it Works — Operational Summary

1. Vyyb identifies a vendor partner with a strategic pitch location (estate entrance, matatu stage, university gate, office park)
2. Vyyb provides: cart (facilitated), daily Hive supply at the agreed supply price, training, brand, Vyyb OS access
3. Vendor provides: location, labour, gas cylinders, local permits
4. Vendor pays: supply price per batch ordered + monthly flat fee
5. Vendor earns: 100% of their retail sales revenue, keeping the spread above supply price

### Why this Works

- Vendor has skin in the game — they run the outlet, they serve their community, they keep all their sales upside
- Vyyb retains quality control via Hive supply + QR batch audit — no outside sourcing is permitted
- Sustena tracks demand, predicts logistics, and assists vendor in knowing how much to order — reducing guesswork and waste
- Scales fast — add a cart = add a vendor = grow without property capex
- No shift requirement imposed on vendor: Sustena predicts demand windows; vendor chooses their service hours within the system's guidance
- Flat fee structure means Vyyb's admin burden per vendor is low — no per-sale reconciliation needed

### Vyyb.sustain — Mirrors the Vendor Model from Start

The first Vyyb-owned outlet operates on the same systems, data flows, and rules as a vendor cart will. Every data point from Phase 1 — dispatch times, waste logs, demand patterns, scan events — trains the Vyyb.sustain model so that when vendor partners join, the system already has operational intelligence to support them. Phase 1 also establishes the actual Hive unit economics that will set the supply price and flat fee for Vendor #2.

### Vendor Partner: Criteria

- Located at a commuter chokepoint with consistent foot traffic
- Willing to adhere to Vyyb supply-chain system (QR scan protocol, batch code rules, no outside sourcing)
- Can follow finish-cook procedures: frying, griddle finishing, assembly
- No requirement to run specific shifts — service hours are vendor-determined, informed by Sustena demand prediction
- Agrees to flat fee terms and supply price schedule (formalized in a simple vendor agreement)

## 8. CIRCULAR ECONOMY — FOOD WASTE PROGRAM

Food waste is both a cost and a data signal. Vyyb tracks it from Day 1 and converts it into community value.

### End-of-Day Process

1. Cart staff scans remaining container QR codes at close → Vyyb OS logs remaining quantities by item and batch
2. Hub reviews waste log daily — adjusts next day's dispatch quantities (demand learning)
3. Remaining food is categorised:

| Category | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Whole batch (e.g. unsold biryani, rice) | Allocated to registered community partner at fraction price |
| Leftover tortilla bases + eggs | Assembled into **Egg Slices** at cart or Hub next morning (discounted item) |
| Leftover morning rice | Mixed at Hub into **Discounted Fried Rice Mix** with peas, spring onion, carrot, egg |
| Leftover mandazi / chapati | Return to Hub — repurpose as Egg Slice base or staff meal |
| Items past safe hold time | Logged as waste, disposed per food safety rules |

### Community Partners

- Registered with Vyyb OS (eventually Sustena's community registry)
- Receive end-of-day batches at **cost price or below** (no margin — social dividend)
- Examples: soup kitchens, school feeding programs, community initiatives
- All transactions logged as circular economy events — data for ESG/impact reporting and investor narrative

### Brand Value

This program is visible. The QR label on every container can link to a "where does leftover Vyyb go?" page — turning food waste reduction into a brand story and community loyalty driver.
