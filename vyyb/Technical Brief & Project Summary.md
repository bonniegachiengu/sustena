### 1. System Overview

The project is a **6-Module Interlocking Mobile Kitchen** with a total assembled footprint of approximately **3m × 2m**. When closed, it forms an armored, secure, weatherproof steel structure — a sealed kibanda. When deployed, it opens into a fully functional modular finishing kitchen with an enclosed interior workspace.

**Assembly geometry:** The four ground-level modules (1–4) form a rectangle. Modules 1 and 3 are the two long faces (each 3m). Modules 2 and 4 are the two short sides that close off the ends. The result is an enclosed interior workspace approximately 3m long × 1m deep (accounting for module wall thickness). Module 5 (solar roof and awning) covers the top. Module 6 (floor panels) bridges the interior workspace floor.

**Security principle:** All interlocking latches, toggle clamps, and connecting bolts are on the **interior face** of each module. When the structure is closed and locked from inside before shutdown, there are no accessible fasteners on the exterior — the outside presents a smooth, cladded surface. The door (Module 4) is the only exterior access point.

### 3. Module Breakdown

#### Module 1 — Front Service & Combi-Counter Unit
*Long face. Customer-facing. Spans full 3m length.*

- **Lower section:** Insulated cavity housing a solar-powered under-counter DC compressor refrigerator (12V/24V).
- **Countertop:** Recessed cooled salad well with Grade 304 GN (gastro-norm) pan inserts, adjacent to an insulated wet-element Bain Marie cavity for hot-holding bases and stews.
- **Upper structure:** Glass-enclosed insulated heated display case (visual merchandising — protein and base display). Remaining flat workspace accommodates a mounted POS tablet inside a welded steel lockbox.
- **Branding face:** Full exterior Alucobond panel surface for vinyl brand wrap. Primary customer-facing signage face.

---

#### Module 2 — Hydro-Station
*Short side. One end of the rectangle.*

- **Sink:** Grade 304 SS double-basin sink with faucet.
- **Water supply (gravity-fed):** Fresh water tank (50–100L) mounted at the top of the module frame. Gravity pressure feeds the faucet — no pump required. Tank is fixed in position (not rolled out). Fill via external cap.
- **Waste collection (external cage, see Section 4):** Greywater does NOT collect under the sink. The siphon drains through the module floor to the external waste tank cage. This frees the under-sink interior for dry storage shelving.
- **Under-sink storage:** Dry goods and packaging storage — shelves or drawers, unlocked during service.
- **Utility pass-through:** 12V/24V DC marine plug terminals for electrical connections between modules.

---

#### Module 3 — Modular Back-Bar Cook Station
*Long face. Staff-facing (interior). Same 3m length as Module 1.*

- **Countertop:** Standardized rectangular drop-in cutout sections across the full 3m run, with custom flush SS lids when appliances are not in use.
- **Appliance trays:** Fabricate uniform SS drop-in plates for the following appliances — all plates must fit identically into the counter cutouts for hot-swap flexibility:
  - Countertop double-basket gas fryer
  - Gas flat-top griddle (400–500mm)
  - 2-burner gas range
  - Commercial countertop juicer
- **Utility terminals:** Brass quick-disconnect gas couplings for LPG lines (connect to external cylinder cage, Module 2 side). Heavy-duty 12V/24V DC marine plugs for electrical appliances.
- **Lower section:** Uncooled lockable cupboards for storing swapped-out appliances and cooking tools.
- **Overhead:** Lightweight aluminum smoke extraction hood and chimney canopy, welded to the top frame of Module 3. Spans full cooking run length.

---

#### Module 4 — Door Module
*Short side. Other end of the rectangle. Primary security and access point.*

- **Structure:** Full-height reinforced door panel that closes and locks the assembled kitchen from one end. When the four ground modules are assembled, Module 4 is the only way in or out.
- **Locking:** Heavy-duty multi-point locking mechanism, keyed or padlocked from outside. Interior latch bar for staff use during service.
- **Access at night:** When the structure is shut down, Module 4 is locked last from outside. The interior is then sealed — all equipment, the POS tablet, and stock inside are secured behind a steel-faced, Alucobond-clad door.
- **Ventilation panel (optional):** A louvred steel vent panel at the top of Module 4 for passive air circulation when closed overnight — does not compromise security.

---

#### Module 5 — Solar Roof & Customer Awning
*Overhead. Attached via telescopic legs to all four corner modules.*

- **Main canopy frame:** Over-arching standalone frame sheeted with Alucobond. Manual tilt-adjustment hinge brackets on the rear slope for mounting a 300W–400W monocrystalline solar panel at optimal angle.
- **Telescopic support legs:** Four legs that extend and slide into socket brackets welded to the outer top corners of Modules 1–4. Secured with heavy-duty steel safety pins. Setup and takedown without tools.
- **Customer awning:** Attached to the front edge of the canopy via a heavy-duty piano hinge. Extends outward over Module 1 (the front service counter) using automotive gas struts or manual drop-rods. Forms a customer rain shelter when deployed.
- **Awning underside:** Print the Till Number and dynamic M-Pesa QR code in high-visibility on the awning underside — visible to customers queuing at the counter, sheltered from rain.
- **Integrated 12V DC lighting:** LED strip rail along the inner canopy frame edge for counter and workspace illumination. All 12V DC — no inverter needed.

---

#### Module 6 — Non-Slip Interior Floor Panels
*Base. Bridges the enclosed interior workspace floor.*

- **Purpose:** Creates a clean, non-slip, food-safe working floor inside the enclosed rectangle between Modules 1–4.
- **Material:** Heavy-duty aluminium tread plate (checker plate) or commercial-grade rubber/PVC interlocking anti-fatigue panels, cut to fit the interior workspace footprint.
- **Drainage:** Panels should have a slight fall toward one corner or a central drainage gap above a grease trap or drain point if the pitch location has drainage.
- **Edges:** Bevelled or rubber-tipped edges where panels meet module bases — no trip hazard, no food-trap gap.
- **Transport:** Panels stack or interlock for compact transport alongside the modules.

### 4. External Utility Cages

These two cage structures are fabricated as part of the overall build. They attach to the exterior corners of the assembled structure — the corners where the modules connect create an unavoidable external angle, and these cages are designed to make productive use of that space.

#### Gas Cylinder Cage
*Location: Exterior corner — Module 2 (Hydro) side, overlapping the Module 2 / Module 1 or Module 3 junction.*

- **Structure:** Welded GI square tube grid cage, sized to house 2 × 13kg LPG cylinders (or 4 × 6kg cylinders) in an upright, secured position.
- **Access:** A hinged, lockable grid-door (padlocked) on the outer face for cylinder swap-out without opening the main kitchen.
- **Gas line:** Flexible LPG hose from cylinder regulators routes through a grommetted port into Module 3's quick-disconnect coupling terminal.
- **Ventilation:** Open grid construction — no buildup of LPG vapour.
- **Casters:** The cage base sits on the ground on two heavy-duty casters (aligned with the module casters) so the whole assembled structure rolls as one unit.

#### Water Tank Cage
*Location: Exterior corner — opposite side (Module 4/Door side).*

- **Fresh water tank:** 50–100L tank fixed at the top of the cage frame. Fill cap accessible from the exterior. Gravity feeds into Module 2's Hydro-Station sink faucet via a fixed pipe route.
- **Waste/greywater tank:** 20–40L jerrican or fabricated SS tank, housed in the lower section of the cage. A hinged, lockable grid-gate on the outer face allows the waste tank to be rolled or lifted out for emptying — tool-free swap.
- **Under-sink connection:** The sink siphon in Module 2 routes through the module floor via a short pipe directly to the waste tank — the waste tank does NOT sit under the sink inside the module. Interior is kept clear for storage.
- **Casters:** Same as gas cage — base casters aligned with module casters for unified rolling.

### 5. Power System

All DC — no inverter.

| Component | Spec | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| Solar panel | 300W–400W monocrystalline | Mounted on Module 5 roof, manual tilt |
| Charge controller | 30A MPPT | Under-counter in Module 1 or Module 3 lower section |
| Battery | 100Ah–150Ah LiFePO4 lithium OR 200Ah Solar Gel | LiFePO4 preferred — lighter, longer cycle life |
| Distribution | 12V DC fused bus bar | Feeds: fridge, heated display, POS tablet, LED lighting, Wi-Fi router |
| Cooking power | LPG gas only | Fryer, griddle, gas range — no electric cooking |
| **Estimated solar system cost** | **KES 44,000–58,000** | Luthuli Avenue or Nyamakima sourcing |

### 6. Cashless & Anti-Leakage System

- **POS:** Android tablet, locked in welded steel lockbox on Module 1 countertop.
- **Payment flow:** Order placed → M-Pesa STK Push sent to customer's phone via Daraja API → `SUCCESS` callback received → POS prints/displays order token → kitchen begins preparation. No cash handling at point of sale.
- **QR code placement:** Till Number and dynamic M-Pesa QR code printed on the underside of the customer awning — visible to queuing customers, protected from rain.
- **Wi-Fi:** 4G LTE SIM router (Safaricom/Airtel), battery-backed. Powers POS, Sustena sync, QR batch scanning.

## Part 2: Operational Playbook

### Morning Setup Sequence

1. Roll modules into position and align on plot
2. Lock all internal toggle latches and drop-pins between Module 1–4 corners
3. Lay Module 6 floor panels across interior workspace
4. Attach external utility cages (gas and water) to corners
5. Extend Module 5 telescopic legs into corner socket brackets — secure safety pins
6. Connect LPG hoses to quick-disconnect couplings on Module 3
7. Confirm water tank level — top up if needed
8. Deploy customer awning via gas struts or drop-rods
9. Boot POS tablet, verify Daraja API connection, check solar charge parameters
10. Heated display on — confirm Bain Marie temps at 70°C+, fridge at ≤5°C

### Midnight Shutdown Sequence

1. Shut down LPG cylinders at the regulator — disconnect quick-couplings
2. Retract customer awning and lock into roof position
3. Lower Module 5 roof legs — roof drops to closed/lock position over modules
4. Remove and stack Module 6 floor panels
5. Lock POS tablet inside counter lockbox — tablet stays inside overnight
6. Empty greywater waste tank via cage gate
7. Lock water tank cage gate and gas cylinder cage gate
8. Staff exit through Module 4 — lock from outside
9. **Leave the DC fridge breaker ON** — battery maintains cold storage overnight
