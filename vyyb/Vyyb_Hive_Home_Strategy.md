# VYYB HIVE — HOME KITCHEN STRATEGY
**Version 1.0 — May 2026 · PHASE 1 PLANNING**

> This document explores renting a residential property with a suitable kitchen as the Phase 1 Vyyb Hive, in place of a dedicated commercial kitchen lease. Cross-reference: `Vyyb_Phase1_Cart_Strategy.md` (budget), `Vyyb_QSR_Operations_Strategy.md` (full Hive vision).

---

## 1. THE CORE IDEA

Instead of renting a commercial kitchen space and equipping it from scratch, Phase 1 rents a **residential home with a large, suitable kitchen** — then upgrades and equips that kitchen to commissary standard.

The difference in starting position is significant:
- **Commercial kitchen route:** You lease a bare shell in an industrial or mixed-use zone, install everything (plumbing, drainage, ventilation, power), and pay commercial-rate rent from Day 1.
- **Home kitchen route:** You lease a furnished or semi-furnished house with an existing kitchen layout — existing sink, drainage, gas connection points, basic utilities. You add commissary equipment into a space that is already habitable and functional.

The home also gives you a place to live or house a full-time Hive staff member, which reduces the effective rent cost further if the operational savings are factored against the total accommodation cost.

## 2. WHAT THE IDEAL PROPERTY LOOKS LIKE

The property must meet a minimum threshold to function as a Hive. These are non-negotiable:

### Kitchen Requirements

| Requirement | Minimum Spec | Notes |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Kitchen floor area | 12–20 m² | Enough for 5–6 workstations + clearance aisles |
| Ceiling height | 2.5m+ | Required for ventilation hood clearance above fryer/grill |
| Drainage | Working sink drain + drain point on floor or routable to outside | Grease trap can be installed inline |
| Water supply | Reliable running water (town council supply or borehole) | No water supply = no commissary |
| Gas access | LPG cylinder points OR existing gas piping | External cylinders + copper pipe work |
| Electrical supply | 240V AC (standard Kenya) | For fridges, freezers, blenders, convection oven |
| Ventilation | Window(s) or existing roof ventilation above cooking area | Supplemented by extraction hood — must have an exit point for fume ducting |
| Floor | Ceramic tile or concrete — no carpet or wood | Washable, food-safe surface |

### Property Requirements

| Requirement | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| Compound/gate | Gated property for nightly security — the outlet modules may also be stored here |
| Parking/driveway | Enough space to park a boda or 1-ton pickup for dispatch at 05:30 |
| Location | Within 30–45 minutes of the outlet pitch location (Mirema Drive area or target zone) — dispatch at 05:15, arrive 06:00 |
| Landlord consent | Must allow commercial food production on premises — this is critical. Get it in writing. |
| Plot size | Ideally enough exterior space to store/assemble modules when not deployed |

### Ideal Property Types

These property types in Nairobi's middle-belt tend to fit:

- **Maisonette with large kitchen (Ruaka, Githurai, Kasarani, Mirema area):** Larger kitchens, compounds, moderate rents. Good proximity to Mirema Drive outlet zone.
- **Bungalow with separate kitchen annex:** Separate kitchen building = built-in ventilation and noise isolation. Best-case scenario.
- **2-bedroom flat with extended kitchen (ground floor):** Ground floor only — drainage routing and gas cylinder access are easier. Avoid upper floors for a kitchen operation.
- **Commercial-residential hybrid plot (godown + attached house):** If available in budget — the godown houses equipment and outlet modules, the house provides living quarters + basic kitchen. Ideal endgame for Phase 1.

## 3. WHAT YOU UPGRADE (HOME → HIVE)

The kitchen arrives as a domestic space. These upgrades convert it:

### Non-Negotiable Upgrades

| Upgrade | Cost Estimate | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| SS workbench × 1 (Jua Kali, 1.5m) | 4,000–8,000 | Core prep surface — domestic counters are usually insufficient |
| Ventilation extraction hood + ducting (above cooking station) | 8,000–20,000 | Duct through wall or roof. Hardest physical install — check ceiling height first |
| Grease trap (inline, below sink drain) | 5,000–15,000 | Non-negotiable for any cooking oil operation. Prevents drain blockage |
| LPG cylinder manifold + copper piping to cooking station | 5,000–12,000 | Gas fitter, 1-day job |
| Non-slip floor mats / rubber tile | 3,000–8,000 | Safety compliance |
| Commercial SS sink (if domestic sink insufficient) | 0–18,000 | Assess on-site — existing sink may be usable |
| Fire extinguishers (Class K × 1 + ABC × 1) | 6,000–12,000 | At cooking station + general |
| Handwash station (if not separate from prep sink) | 1,000–3,000 | Food hygiene requirement |

**Upgrade subtotal: ~32,000–96,000 KES** (vs. fitting out a bare commercial shell from scratch at 3–5×)

### Deferred to Month 2–3

- Chest freezer (defer Day 1 if budget is tight — use upright fridge freezer compartment for first 4–6 weeks)
- QR batch label printer (defer 2–4 weeks — hand-written labels acceptable for the first dispatch runs)
- Fire suppression system (extinguishers satisfy Phase 1; suppression system belongs in Phase 2 commercial Hive)

## 6. LEGAL & COMPLIANCE IMPLICATIONS

### What Changes Under the Home Kitchen Model

The home kitchen is not a registered commercial kitchen. This has compliance implications:

| Issue | Risk Level | Mitigation |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Food Hygiene License | **CRITICAL** — Nairobi County requires a food hygiene certificate for any premises preparing food for commercial sale | Apply for the Mobile Food Service Code 500 bundle at nairobiservices.go.ke, which covers mobile operations. The kitchen may need a separate County Public Health inspection to operate as a commissary — confirm with your local public health officer. |
| Landlord agreement | **HIGH** — operating a commercial kitchen without landlord consent is a lease breach and can result in eviction | Get explicit written consent in the tenancy agreement or a side letter. Frame it clearly: you are a food production business operating from this residential property. |
| Neighbours / noise | **MEDIUM** — 03:30 kitchen start time will generate noise. An isolated bungalow or ground-floor unit is better than a flat in a busy block. | Choose property with minimal shared walls. Discuss with neighbors early. Avoid ground-floor in a dense apartment block. |
| LPG safety | **MEDIUM** — residential LPG supply setups are not typically rated for commercial cylinder banks | Use licensed gas fitter for copper pipe install. Store cylinders outside (exterior cage or compound cage, not inside the kitchen). Standard residential LPG setup handles 2–4 × 13kg cylinders when installed correctly. |
| Greywater / waste | **LOW–MEDIUM** — commercial cooking volumes produce grease-heavy wastewater. A grease trap is non-negotiable. | Install inline grease trap below sink drain before starting operations. Empty regularly. |

### Practical Path

1. Identify the property.
2. Confirm landlord consent in writing before signing lease.
3. Book a pre-inspection with the local Nairobi County Public Health office to walk through requirements for using the property as a food production premises.
4. Install upgrades (hood, grease trap, LPG piping, workbenches) before the health inspection.
5. Apply for Food Hygiene License via the County eServices portal alongside the outlet's Mobile Food Service Code 500 permit.

## 7. OPERATIONAL FIT ASSESSMENT

### What the Home Hive Can Handle (Phase 1 Volumes)

Phase 1 is a **single outlet** with an estimated daily production run of:
- 50–150 portions of fried/grilled chicken (scaled to demand)
- 20–40 portions of biryani
- Sauces, tortilla bases, mandazi dough, samosa pastry — batch prep 3× per week
- Drinks concentrate, Vyyb rubs — batch prep once weekly

A standard 15–20 m² kitchen with the 9-zone Hive equipment spec (Section 4 of Phase 1 strategy) comfortably handles these volumes. The home kitchen is a Phase 1 production facility, not a Phase 2 multi-outlet commissary — volume at single-outlet scale does not require industrial infrastructure.

### What the Home Hive Cannot Do (Phase 2 Triggers)

When any of these happen, it's time to move to a dedicated commercial kitchen:
- **3+ vendor partner outlets** being supplied simultaneously — dispatch logistics outgrow a residential compound
- **Production volume doubles** — Phase 1 single outlet exceeds projected volumes, or Phase 2 outlets added faster than expected
- **Nairobi County escalates** — County health officers mandate commercial premises for the food production license
- **Cold storage is a bottleneck** — a domestic-grade property cannot house the refrigeration units needed for multi-outlet cold chain
- **Staff count grows beyond 4 in the kitchen** — a 15–20m² kitchen safely fits 3–4 staff; beyond that, a Phase 2 commissary kitchen is required

## 9. DECISION CHECKLIST — HOME HIVE GO / NO-GO

Before committing to a specific property, confirm all of the following:

- [ ] Kitchen floor area ≥ 12m²
- [ ] Ceiling height ≥ 2.5m (measure above intended fryer/grill zone)
- [ ] Existing floor drain or drain point in kitchen floor, OR drain can be routed through wall to exterior
- [ ] Running water confirmed — turn the taps on during viewing
- [ ] Electrical supply confirmed — check fuse box, confirm at least one 32A circuit available
- [ ] Compound/gate confirmed — gated, secure, enough space for vehicle access at 05:30
- [ ] Landlord consent obtained in writing — commercial food production permitted
- [ ] Ventilation exit point confirmed — window, vent, or routable ducting path for extraction hood
- [ ] Location within 30–45 min of target outlet pitch (test drive in morning traffic)
- [ ] No shared kitchen walls with neighbouring bedrooms or units that would be disturbed by 03:30 start

## 10. PHASE 1 TIMELINE IMPACT

Switching to the home Hive approach **accelerates Phase 1** rather than delaying it. The bottleneck on commercial kitchen setup is:
1. Finding a commercial space at the right price (takes 4–8 weeks in Nairobi's commercial-kitchen rental market)
2. Negotiating commercial lease terms (often 3-month deposits, fit-out clauses)
3. Fit-out and inspection (4–6 weeks after lease signing)

The home kitchen route:
1. House viewings and selection: 1–2 weeks
2. Lease signing with landlord: 1 week
3. Kitchen upgrade installation (hood, grease trap, LPG, workbenches): 1–2 weeks (all Jua Kali / plumber, no long-lead items)
4. Hive equipment procurement and delivery: 1–2 weeks parallel
5. County health pre-inspection and Food Hygiene License: submit Day 1, approval 2–4 weeks

**Realistic home Hive to service-ready: 4–6 weeks** from property selection to first production run.
